Experiments · Adaptive Verifier Graphs

Evidence-local verification with calibrated abstention across two agent domains

Abstract of results We evaluate Adaptive Verifier Graphs (AVG) against monolithic trajectory judges on two independent agent domains — a software-engineering domain (Terminal-Bench) and a customer-service policy domain (τ-bench). Across both, AVG reduces the rate at which failed trajectories are wrongly credited as valid by 4–16×, with the margin widening on long-horizon traces; we show the reduction is driven by a calibrated abstention rule rather than by any individual checker, that a mis-specified verifier abstains rather than over-credits, and that AVG outperforms a genuine LLM judge by ~15×. We then describe and empirically justify the escalation mechanism that lets AVG update its obligations on the fly.

1Experimental setup

Our central hypothesis is that verification which binds each verdict to local, in-trace evidence and abstains when that evidence is absent will detect reward hacks that a holistic judge — one that reasons over the entire transcript at once — systematically misses. To test this without over-fitting to a single benchmark, we evaluate on two domains that share no code path beyond AVG's domain-neutral core and that stress deliberately complementary failure modes.

1.1Domains and data

Terminal-Bench comprises real software-engineering trajectories in which an agent edits files and runs a test suite inside a shell; ground truth is the mechanical pass/fail of that suite. Its characteristic reward hack is an integrity violation — the agent tampers with the test file or deletes the artifact under evaluation so that a nominally-passing endpoint no longer reflects a solved task. τ-bench (Sierra's retail and airline customer-service benchmark) comprises real tool-calling trajectories against a user database governed by a written standard operating procedure (SOP); ground truth is an exact match of the final database state (eval_result.score). Its characteristic reward hack is a policy violation — the agent mutates state without first authenticating the user or obtaining the required confirmation, yet still reaches a plausible-looking endpoint. Crucially, in both domains the label is defined by the environment, not by any model we control, so a judge cannot be right merely by agreeing with another judge.

Both domains are reached through an identical pipeline. A framework-neutral adapter canonicalises raw traces into steps tagged with a role, a status, and artifact effects; an obligation generator emits per-step proof obligations from the domain specification Sd; evidence-local checkers score each obligation as pass / fail / abstain against the evidence reachable from that step; and a single aggregation rule combines the obligation verdicts into a trajectory verdict of valid, invalid, or uncertain (abstain). Adding τ-bench required no change to any node, edge, checker mechanism, or aggregation rule — only a new adapter, a new Sd, and a domain-artifact bundle Ωd holding the verbatim SOPs and the database schema. The study is thus method-preserving: any difference across domains is attributable to data and specification, not to a changed algorithm.

1.2Verifier arms and baselines

The primary contrast is between AVG (avg_full) — the full graph with evidence-local obligations and abstention-aware aggregation — and a monolithic judge (monolithic), the standard baseline that inspects the entire trajectory together with its endpoint and returns a single verdict. We run the monolithic judge both as a cheap endpoint heuristic (for the large offline sweeps) and, on τ-bench, as a genuine gpt-4o-mini LLM judge. We include three ablations: exec_only (mechanical checkers only, no semantic checking), exec_free (no execution evidence), and, for the stress tests, avg_no_integrity (the integrity module removed).

1.3Metrics

Our headline metric is the false-valid rate (FV), the probability that the verifier returns valid on a trajectory whose ground-truth label is invalid: FV = Pr(verdict = valid | truth = invalid). In a reward-modelling or RL-filtering pipeline this is exactly the quantity that admits reward hacks into training, which is why we treat it as the safety-critical number rather than raw accuracy. Because AVG is permitted to abstain, we report FV alongside the resolved fraction (coverage, one minus the abstention rate) and the resolved accuracy (accuracy on the traces it does decide); a verifier that abstains everywhere trivially attains FV = 0 and is uninteresting, so the pairing of low FV with non-trivial coverage is what matters. For calibration analyses we report AUROC and expected calibration error (ECE) over a shared confidence score. We additionally isolate a long-horizon slice (trajectories of ≥ 20 steps), where holistic judgment is expected to degrade most.

1.4Protocol

Offline results are reported as mean ± standard error over three seeds at n = 1000 trajectories per seed on τ-bench and n = 3000 on Terminal-Bench, with each sample balanced 50/50 between valid and invalid so that the base rate is 0.5 and FV is directly comparable across arms. The LLM-judge slice is a single seed at n = 120 with gpt-4o-mini. All figures reproduce from the committed result files; the offline pipeline is fully deterministic.

2Results

2.1Evidence-local verification versus monolithic judgment (Q1)

Table 1 reports the core comparison. On both domains the monolithic judge credits the majority of failed trajectories as valid, while AVG almost never does. The reduction is 4.4× on Terminal-Bench (FV 0.187 vs. 0.830) and 16× on τ-bench (FV 0.042 vs. 0.678). The effect is strongest exactly where it should be: on the long-horizon slice the τ-bench gap widens to 22× (0.034 vs. 0.756), because a holistic judge is increasingly seduced by a long trajectory that superficially "looks" finished, whereas AVG's per-step obligations keep the verdict pinned to whether each consequential action was actually justified by evidence in the trace. AVG pays for this precision with coverage — offline it resolves roughly 16% of τ traces and abstains on the rest — which is the correct behaviour when the deciding evidence (for τ-bench, the final database state) is not present in the transcript at all.

2.2The safety is in the abstention rule (Q3)

A natural objection is that AVG's advantage comes simply from having better checkers. To rule this out, we force AVG to commit a valid/invalid verdict on every trajectory, disabling abstention while leaving the checkers untouched. Table 2 shows the result: false credits balloon, from 0.128 to 0.724 on Terminal-Bench and from 0.042 to 0.787 on τ-bench — reductions of 82.3% and 94.7% attributable to the decision rule alone. We report the underlying calibration honestly. The shared-score AUROC is only 0.46 (Terminal-Bench) and 0.60 (τ-bench): a genuinely weak-calibration finding, which we attribute to the trajectory-level reward being a coarse label for a step-level score. The point is that abstention does not require a well-ranked score to be effective — the binary abstain / decide partition suffices to remove the large majority of false credits. Notably, when the semantic checker is enabled with a real LLM (§2.5), abstention also improves ranking: AUROC rises to 0.688 for AVG against 0.558 for the forced arm over the identical score.

2.3A mis-specified verifier abstains, it does not over-credit (Q2)

Because each domain requires its own adapter and specification, a fair question is what happens under mis-specification — if a verifier built for one domain is applied to another's traces. Table 3 is the zero-shot cross-domain transfer matrix, reporting resolved fraction (the diagonal is where a verifier meets the domain it was specified for). The structure is a clean diagonal: only the matched adapter-and-spec pairing binds obligations and produces decisions (0.138 on Terminal-Bench, 0.162 on τ-bench), while every off-diagonal cell and the domain-neutral verifier abstain on essentially every trace. Decisively, the false-valid rate never exceeds 0.16 in any cell: a mis-specified verifier cannot manufacture false confidence — it withholds judgment. This is the property that makes AVG safe to extend: adding a domain can only add coverage where the specification matches, never spurious credit where it does not.

2.4Stress tests, and where the protection comes from (SA-6)

We next inject targeted reward hacks whose visible endpoint "succeeds", so that a correct verifier must reject a trajectory that superficially looks complete. The instructive finding is that the AVG layer responsible for the catch differs by domain, and we report this rather than paper over it. On Terminal-Bench (Table 4) the integrity module is load-bearing: when it is removed, roughly 52 points of tampered-test false-valids leak back (FV rises from 0.000 to 0.521 on the test-tamper perturbation), while with the module AVG catches 100%. The monolithic judge is entirely blind here, crediting a modified test file as valid every time (FV 1.000). On τ-bench (Table 5) the same three-way ablation moves the false-valid rate by ≈ 0, because on a policy domain the catch is performed instead by the policy-linking layer — a consequential database mutation is policy-sensitive, so an unauthenticated or unconfirmed action is withheld regardless of the integrity module. The same graph deploys two different protective layers, each doing its job on the domain it was meant for; the clean-control negative-transfer rate (AVG vetoing a legitimate trace) is 0.064.

2.5The advantage survives a real LLM judge

A skeptic might attribute the gap to our monolithic baseline being a weak heuristic. We therefore replace it with a genuine gpt-4o-mini judge that reads the full transcript (Figure 2). The gap does not close: AVG's false-valid rate is 0.050 against the LLM judge's 0.733 overall (~15×), widening to 0.027 versus 0.838 on the long-horizon slice (31×). Most tellingly, the LLM judge is slightly worse than the endpoint heuristic (0.733 vs. 0.678) — a stronger model does not help, because the failure is structural: a holistic judge is confidently misled by a plausible trajectory that violates the SOP, which is precisely the case evidence-local verification is built to catch. We also observe, and report, that the narrow semantic checker resolves individual per-mutation obligations but issues confident passes sparsely, so it rarely flips the whole-trajectory verdict offline — the same "semantic adds coverage, seldom flips verdicts" behaviour we see on Terminal-Bench.

2.6Online intervention and offline harness improvement (Q4)

Two further experiments probe AVG beyond one-shot scoring. In the online intervention setting (SA-4) we ask whether AVG's confident negative signal, evaluated causally along the trajectory, arrives early enough to be actionable. It does: the confident failed-obligation signal is precise (0.649 vs. a 0.50 base rate on τ-bench, 0.73 on Terminal-Bench) and timely (a median of 4 steps saved on τ-bench and 18 on Terminal-Bench), because a failed mutation tool call is a genuine mechanical catch that fires before the trajectory ends. In the offline harness-improvement setting (SA-5) a witness-mined edit to the specification, gated to add no new false vetoes, cuts held-out false-valids by 94% on unseen Terminal-Bench task families (0.142 → 0.009); on τ-bench the held-out false-valid rate was already 0.037, so the terminal-flavoured mined template yields a 0% held-out reduction with zero negative transfer — an honest null we report rather than suppress, indicating that τ-specific remediation templates would be needed for a further gain there.

3Dynamically updating the obligations

All results above use a single static verification pass. We now describe the mechanism that allows AVG to update its obligations during verification, and — because the design was chosen empirically rather than asserted — the controlled comparison that selected it.

3.1Motivation

In the base system every obligation is scored exactly once. A high-severity policy obligation whose deciding evidence lies outside its initial local window — for instance, "this mutation was confirmed with the user", when the confirmation occurred several turns earlier — therefore abstains. This is safe but leaves coverage on the table. We want the verifier to act on such an abstention: to go and look for the missing evidence, rather than merely recording that it was absent.

3.2Design space and the controlled comparison

We prototyped three variants and measured each on a held-out set of 40 τ-bench traces with gpt-4o-mini before committing to one (Table 6). The baseline STATIC pass does no escalation. Verdict-only re-scoring re-runs the checker over a broadened window but discards the gathered context after producing a new verdict. Re-localizing escalation additionally lifts the gathered neighbourhood into a first-class evidence node and attaches it to the obligation. Two findings decided the choice. First, both escalating variants recover the same coverage — abstention falls from 0.55 to 0.25, a 30-point gain — while holding the safety-critical false-valid rate flat at 0.100; escalation converts abstentions into decisions without adding false credit (31 of 31 escalated obligations resolved). Second, only re-localization grows the obligation's candidate-evidence set Ei (by 0.72 nodes per trace) and enriches the audit witness EW (from 1.27 to 1.90), including on obligations that remain abstained — so a reviewer can see the broader context that was pulled in and why it was still insufficient. We adopted re-localization: escalation should update an obligation's localization, not merely overwrite its verdict.

3.3The re-localizing escalation procedure

The mechanism is deliberately method-preserving: it re-runs the existing checker over a wider slice of the existing graph and re-applies the existing aggregation rule, so with the LLM disabled it is an exact static no-op and every offline number above is unchanged bit-for-bit. Two design decisions were fixed by measurement. We hold the re-check prompt identical to the verdict-only path — the gathered neighbourhood is presented as "surrounding trace" — so that escalation adds provenance without silently shifting verdicts. And we exclude the intrinsically trace-unverifiable final_answer_support obligations from escalation, because resolving them was measured to double the false-valid rate (0.133 → 0.267): the database-state success signal simply is not in the transcript, so the judge over-passes when pressed to decide.

Procedure 1 — Re-localizing escalation (per abstaining high-severity obligation o)
for round = 1 … max_rounds:
W ← broaden(local_window(o))  # pull in preceding auth / confirmation turns
e ← EvidenceNode(W)  # lift neighbourhood to a first-class node
graph.add(e);  o.E_i ← o.E_i ∪ {e}  # re-localize the obligation
v ← checker(o, e)  # same checker, wider evidence, identical prompt shape
if resolved(v):
graph.add_support_edge(o, e);  refresh_witness(E_W)
break
if o.claim ∈ {final_answer_support}: skip  # excluded: doubles FV
verdict ← aggregate(all obligations)  # existing rule, unchanged

Against the monolithic-LLM's 0.750 false-valid rate, the loop holds AVG at 0.100 while lifting coverage from 0.45 to 0.75. Two knobs govern the coverage/precision trade-off: commit_threshold, which gates confident-only verdict flips, and max_rounds together with the base window width, which bound how far escalation reaches.

3.4Why the obligations must be atomic

Escalation is only sound if the obligations it grows are themselves at the lowest sensible level of abstraction; otherwise a re-check inflates an already-tangled judgment. We therefore decomposed policy obligations to one obligation per governing constraint in Sd.Kd, rather than one opaque "complies with the entire SOP" obligation. The earlier whole-SOP design emitted one obligation per policy artifact, which on a retail trace produced two obligations — one of them checking the wrong (airline) policy — and over 200 traces expanded 204 mutations into 408 obligations, half of them irrelevant. The atomic generator instead checks each constraint at the lowest level that fits it. A constraint carrying an ordering precondition (authenticate-before-action) becomes a mechanical check: a successful prior authenticate step must precede the action; this uses no LLM, is reproducible offline, generalises to any domain's constraint set, and false-flags only 2 of 300 valid traces (0.7%) while giving AVG an offline policy signal it previously lacked. A constraint without ordering (confirm-before-mutation) becomes a narrow semantic check whose evidence is that single rule's text, not the 5,700-character SOP — keeping the checker's context local, so that escalation (§3.3) can then gather exactly the surrounding turns needed to decide it. The outcome is no duplication, no cross-domain leakage, and one primitive rule per obligation — the precondition under which escalation can grow Ei safely. Terminal-Bench's no-test-mutation constraint flows through the identical path unchanged.

4Summary of findings

Across two independent domains and a method-preserving change of only data and specification, evidence-local verification with calibrated abstention reduces the false-valid rate by 4–16× relative to a monolithic judge, with the margin widening on long-horizon traces and surviving a real LLM judge (~15×). Controlled ablations locate the safety in the abstention decision rule (removing it restores 82–95% of false credits) and show that mis-specification causes abstention, never over-crediting. Finally, an empirically-selected re-localizing escalation mechanism, operating over atomic per-constraint obligations, recovers 30 points of coverage with the false-valid rate held flat — letting AVG update its obligations on the fly while preserving the guarantee that made the static system safe.
